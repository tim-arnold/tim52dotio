Privacy Policy and Terms of Use

Test Recipients Chrome extension · Effective October 8, 2026

In short: everything you enter stays in your own browser. The extension makes no network requests and sends nothing to the publisher or anyone else. There are no accounts, analytics, ads, or trackers.

What the extension stores

The extension stores only what you enter on its Settings page:

  • Client names, URLs and paths, page-detection settings, send ranges, and colors
  • Recipient groups, including the email addresses you add to them

This data is saved with Chrome's extension storage (chrome.storage.local) on your device, in your Chrome profile. It is not synced to your Google account or other devices. It stays until you delete it in Settings or remove the extension; removing the extension deletes it. The extension does not add its own encryption; the data is protected by your device's and Chrome profile's security.

What the extension reads from web pages

The extension has no standing access to any website. It acts only when you click its toolbar icon, and only on the tab you are viewing. At that moment it:

  • Reads the tab's address to find the matching client
  • If a client matches, reads the text of one element you configured (to detect the send code) and checks whether the recipients field is on the page
  • If no client matches, checks the page for the standard Luminate Online message title and recipients field, so it can offer to set the page up as a client
  • When you click a group button, writes the recipient list into the field you configured

It does not read other page content, record your browsing history, or run in the background. Nothing it reads is stored or sent anywhere.

What is sent or shared

Nothing. The extension makes no network requests of any kind. It does not send data to the publisher, to Google, or to any third party, and it loads no remote code. The publisher never receives or has access to your settings or email addresses.

Things that leave the extension because you asked

  • Export files: Export downloads a JSON file with your settings and email addresses to your computer. Where that file goes afterward, including sharing it with teammates, is up to you. Treat it like any file containing contact information.
  • Filled fields: the recipient list written into a page is then handled by that website (for example, your email service provider) under its own privacy policy.
